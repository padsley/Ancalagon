DRAGON · Ancalagon validation

Ancalagon vs GEANT3

A full-distribution comparison of the GEANT4 port against the GEANT3 DRAGON simulation, for two reactions at the real 2014 hardware tune. It exposed geometry bugs in Ancalagon that destroyed the angle-independent foci at MSLT and FSLT. With them fixed, 15O(α,γ)19Ne transmission rises from 51 % to 96 %, and the first-order ion optics match GEANT3 to within 1 % from Q1 through D2.

Tune dragon_2014_DSSSD, MSLT 0.75 × 1.25 cm Events 10 000 per code per reaction Run 2026-09-25; fixes in Ancalagon main @ b584b92
Resolution

Geometry bugs, found and fixed

Identical single rays fired in both codes and compared at every field-free plane showed Ancalagon already off after Q1–Q2, the first element pair.

Fixed

Quad field origin

Ancalagon placed each quad's field origin at its container centre; the field is defined from the entrance reference plane, as in GEANT3. Every quad sat downstream by half its length plus any drift: 12.6 cm for Q1. Present since the first commit.

Fixed

Overlapping fringe fields

GEANT3 adds the fields of every overlapping element at a point. Geant4 gives a point to one volume, so where neighbouring quads overlap one fringe was silently dropped. Ancalagon now sums them the same way.

Fixed

Dipole fringes cut short

D1's and D2's containers stopped short of their own fringe fields, about 14 cm of D1's 30 cm, so the on-axis ray left D1 1.6 mrad off. Each dipole's field now acts over its full reach, including in the gaps.

Removed

Corrections for the old bugs

A 2.6 % D2 field trim and a Q14 steering kick had been tuned against the buggy optics. With the bugs fixed both pushed the beam away from GEANT3, so they are off by default.

Open

E2 central ray

Ancalagon's E2 bend starts about 6 cm earlier along the path than GEANT3's, which leaves the on-axis ray 5.7 mm apart after E2. Both codes place E2 at the same deck position; GEANT3's e-dipole frame construction is the next suspect.

Where the recoils end up

Final locationGEANT3Ancalagon

Percent of reacting events. Ancalagon reports the volume each recoil stopped in; GEANT3's stopping points are assigned to the nearest element of the same beamline geometry. Ancalagon's MSLT losses split 25.4 % on the X− jaw and 14.4 % on X+.

The mass slit is not an angle focus in Ancalagon

MSLT is designed so that a recoil's position there does not depend on its angle leaving the target. The 19Ne cone reaches about 16 mrad, compared with about 7 mrad for 40Ca in 39K(p,γ)40Ca, so any residual angle term matters much more here. In Ancalagon, MSLT losses rise with angle: 0.6 % below 4 mrad, 8 % at 4–8, 30 % at 8–12 and 55 % beyond 12 mrad.

From each code's own recoilsGEANT3Ancalagon
  • A clean single-ray fan agrees. 19Ne4+ rays fired from past the target gas give (x|θ) ≈ +0.8 mm/mrad at MSLT, with the on-axis ray 1.9 mm off centre. They are exactly linear at Q1, so the error builds up downstream.
  • Fans must start past the target gas. Fired from the target centre, each single ray picks up a random 2–3 mrad multiple-scattering kick in its first step. An earlier 19Ne fan that looked discontinuous was showing exactly that.
  • The tunes differ by 1 %. GEANT3 scales the magnets by 0.9736 and Ancalagon by 0.9641 (RTUN), against 0.07 % for 39K(p,γ)40Ca. A tune offset shifts positions but does not create an angle term, so it does not explain the slope.
  • GEANT3's built-in 15O(α,γ)19Ne cannot be used. Its 0.275 and 0.238 MeV states decay to an undefined particle (95). This comparison uses a file-driven copy, o15ag.dat, with the same levels, lifetimes and branching ratios as Ancalagon.

BGO array

Hit fraction, spectrum shape and crystal multiplicity match. Ancalagon runs about 1 point high on every efficiency, which is 1–2.5σ.

Disagrees

Final focus at FSLT

GEANT3 focuses at FSLT to 0.12 cm rms. Ancalagon is 1.07 cm rms there, driven by an uncorrected (x|θ) = −2.5 mm/mrad.

Efficiencies

GEANT3 efficiencies are per reacting event: about 5 % of its events never react, because the beam crosses the target off resonance. Counting those gives a false 94.5 % transmission. The BGO numbers apply GEANT3's own digitisation to Ancalagon's hits: energy summed per crystal, crystals under 0.1 MeV dropped, highest crystal first.

The final horizontal focus

FSLT is designed as an achromatic focus in the horizontal (dispersive) plane. GEANT3 forms that focus at FSLT. Ancalagon's beam is still converging when it reaches the DSSSD, 66 cm further on. The drift from the end of Q14's fringe field to the DSSSD is field-free, so each recoil's straight-line track gives its position anywhere along it.

What the data say

  • The width is an angle term. A fit of x at FSLT to the initial recoil angle and momentum over 3 000 real recoils gives (x|θ) = −2.53 mm/mrad. It leaves 0.11 cm rms unexplained. Dispersion (about 0.9 cm per 1 % in dp/p) contributes little.
  • Single rays agree with the population. The 40Ca8+ fan is linear at −2.56 mm/mrad, and its on-axis ray lands 3.4 mm off centre.
  • It is not specific to this reaction. 19Ne4+ recoils in 15O(α,γ)19Ne show the same kind of residual angle term, at both MSLT and FSLT (see above).
  • This contradicts the 2026-09-20 validation, which recorded −0.21 mm/mrad at FSLT after the quad-container fix. That figure does not reproduce with real-species rays fired from the target.
  • Consequence: transmission is unaffected only because FSLT and the DSSSD are wide. Ancalagon's DSSSD position and angle distributions should not be used until this is fixed. For 15O(α,γ)19Ne, with its wider cone, the same problem at MSLT does cost transmission (see above).

Phase space at FSLT and the DSSSD

Global coordinates, relative to the beamline axis at x = −1024.717 cm. GEANT3's own ENDV histograms are centred on its ENDV volume, which sits about 3.6 mm from Ancalagon's DSSSD, so they are not used for centroids.

Differences that are not bugs

  • Beam model. Ancalagon fires every recoil from a point with no beam emittance. GEANT3 includes a 1.16 mm rms beam spot, 1.1 mrad beam divergence and a 1.1 cm rms spread of interaction points along the gas target. This is why Ancalagon's vertical spot at the DSSSD is smaller (0.07 against 0.20 cm). The recoil cone itself matches: 5.3 against 5.5 mrad mean.
  • MCP foils. Neither code models them, so they play no part in the difference.

Reproduce

Everything is in validate/g3_crosscheck_k39pg/ in the Ancalagon repository.

./run_g3.sh <workdir>          # 8 × 1250 GEANT3 events, plus 400 in the global frame
./run_ancalagon.sh <workdir>   # 10k reaction events, trajectory extracts, angle fans
python3 analyze.py             # results/summary.txt, compare.png, chart_data.json
  • --track-chain now fires the reaction's own recoil species (RECL Z, A and charge state), not a hard-coded neon ion. Give the matched momentum and fire from the target centre, for example --track-chain 0 0 1320 1 0.0 for k39pg_40ca.
  • GEANT3 run notes. dsbatch truncates FFCARD, INPUT and MITRAY paths at 80 characters. The FOCUSTEST print in src/gustep_mitray.f fires on every recoil step, not only in single-ray runs. Histogram h15 (recoil energy) spans 0–20 MeV and misses these 23 MeV recoils.
